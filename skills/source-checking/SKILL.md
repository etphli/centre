# Source checking

Extract the specific claims to check and identify their time sensitivity. Prefer primary sources, official documentation, original research and the supplied course materials relevant to the task. Use web search only when an actual search tool is available. Without it, state what can be assessed from supplied text and what remains unverified.

Read the source before attributing a claim to it. Check whether it supports the exact assertion, date, population and numerical quantity. Distinguish a primary finding from a review's interpretation. Surface material disagreement and explain uncertainty rather than silently choosing a convenient account.

For each important correction, provide the corrected claim, the source actually consulted and any limitation. Preserve exact URLs supplied by successful tools. Never generate a plausible-looking citation, quote or study title. Keep direct quotations short and use paraphrase for broader synthesis. End with unresolved claims when the evidence does not settle them.
